§ 00 / PRELUDE

There Is No Hard Floor.

Before the rankings, a warning: no single metric guarantees a home run. The hitters who went deep in this sample included guys with profiles you'd otherwise pass over. Treat every threshold below as a probability multiplier, not a gate.

§ 01 / HIGHEST IMPACT

Tier 1The Big Three.

These produced the largest jumps in HR rate when used as filters. If you can only filter on three things, filter on these.

Pull%≥ 45–50%

The strongest single linear signal in the logistic regression. Pulled balls are the ones that leave the yard — they meet the bat squared up and travel in the direction of the shorter foul-pole distance.

At Pull% ≥ 50%, HR rate climbs to 12.2% on a 287-sample. The top standardized coefficient across all metrics.

1.88×Lift at ≥50%
Barrel%≥ 10–12%

The strongest raw correlation with HR outcomes. Barrels are the optimal exit-velocity / launch-angle combinations — by definition, the contact most likely to leave the park.

At Barrel% ≥ 12%, HR rate is 11.3%. Push it to ≥ 20% and you hit 12.4%, but the sample drops fast.

1.74×Lift at ≥12%
Weather Effect≥ 0% (and especially ≥ +5%)

The sneakiest signal in the dataset and the most underweighted by intuition. Wind direction, temperature, and humidity all affect carry — and the effect is non-linear.

At Weather ≥ +10%, the HR rate explodes to 22.4% on n=49 — the single biggest lift of any one-variable filter, though that sample is small enough to warrant some humility.

3.46×Lift at ≥+10%
§ 02 / STRONG SUPPORTING

Tier 2The Confirmers.

Strong supporting filters. Use these to stack on top of Tier 1, or as tiebreakers when the Big Three are roughly even between candidates.

Launch Angle≥ 12–15°

The #2 standardized coefficient in the regression. LA controls for the "ground-ball hitter" failure mode — even an elite barrel rate can't help you if you're pounding it into the dirt. Anything below 10° is a yellow flag.

1.39×Lift at ≥15°
HR/FB≥ 15–20%

A decent rate-of-conversion signal, but partially redundant with Barrel%. At ≥ 20%, HR rate hits 11.6%. Useful for confirming the underlying contact quality is real and repeatable.

1.79×Lift at ≥20%

§ 03 / MEDIUM IMPACT

Tier 3Corroborators.

Don't filter on these alone, but they sharpen the picture when stacked with Tier 1 and Tier 2.

BlastCon%≥ 15–17%

A contact-quality check. At ≥ 17%, HR rate is 9.2%. Useful when paired with Barrel% as a "is the contact actually that clean?" sanity test.

1.41×Lift at ≥17%
Bat Speed≥ 73–75 mph

The raw correlation is weak, but the random forest weighted it more heavily than the linear analysis suggests — meaning it interacts non-linearly with other metrics. At ≥ 75 mph, HR rate climbs to 10.9%.

1.68×Lift at ≥75
§ 04 / LOW IMPACT

Tier 4The Trap.

Don't be fooled. This one looks like it should matter and largely doesn't.

FB% (Fly Ball Rate)— skip as a filter —

The weakest correlation in the dataset (+0.035). Even more telling: the logistic regression assigned FB% a negative coefficient once Pull%, LA, and Barrel% were controlled for.

The interpretation: a high fly-ball rate without the quality-of-contact metrics is empty calories. Lots of pop-ups and warning-track flies. Once you've already filtered on Barrel% and Pull%, FB% adds nothing — and may actively mislead.

~1.0×Effectively none

AND  Weather Effect ≥ 0%

  1. Star Tool flag: Bomb  >  Star  >  unflagged

§ 07 / HONEST CAVEATS

Where This Might Be Wrong.

The analysis is real but the sample is small. Three things to keep in mind as you use this.

01 — SAMPLE SIZE

76 HRs across 3 days is a small sample. Some precise thresholds will shift as you gather more data — especially the Weather ≥ +10% bucket (n=49). Worth re-running this analysis once you have two weeks.

02 — COLLINEARITY

The metrics are correlated with each other. Barrel% ↔ HR/FB, Pull% ↔ LA, and so on. Stacking 5+ filters won't multiply lift — diminishing returns kick in fast around 3–4 filters.

03 — STAR TOOL IS DERIVATIVE

The Star Tool's value comes from agreeing with the underlying metrics. When it disagrees with strong Barrel%/Pull% readings, trust the underlying metrics. Treat the flag as confirmation, not as a primary driver.
