§ 00 / WHAT CHANGED SINCE 5/15

The Real Rankings Emerge.

Adding 5/16 and 5/18 (5/17 unavailable) brought the sample to 2,357 games and 166 HRs. With 60% more data, several rankings have settled — and a few surprises surfaced.

Day-Over-Day Movement
▲ Bat Speed promoted to Tier 1. BatSpd ≥ 75 mph now produces 1.86× lift (was 1.92× before, but on a much larger sample of 282). It's now #1 in random-forest importance and #3 in the regression. Bigger than Barrel%.
▲ Star Tool got dramatically stronger. Bomb-only lift climbed from 2.28× → 2.76×. Star-only from 1.69× → 1.93×. Combined Star+Bomb now 2.31× (was 1.97×). The flag is more reliable than I previously credited.
★ Premium Screen got STRONGER, not weaker. "Bomb + Barrel% ≥ 10%" now yields a 24.64% HR rate at 3.50× lift (was 3.26×). Sample grew to n=69. This is the highest-conviction filter in the dataset.
▼ Barrel% lift compressed. ≥ 20% threshold dropped to 1.68× (was 2.10×). Still useful, but BatSpd has overtaken it as the elite single-metric signal. Filter on both.
▼ Launch Angle lift moderated. Best threshold ≥ 15° now gives 1.33× (was 1.47×). Still independently meaningful in regression, but the headline lift is more modest. LA stays Tier 1 on regression strength, not lift strength.
▼ Weather ≥ +10% continued to soften. Lift dropped from 2.63× → 1.83×. The mean-reversion is consistent — early days were running hot. Still real, just not as dramatic.
▲ FB% climbed out of the trap tier. After 6 days, FB% finally shows a positive regression coefficient (+0.115, was −0.053). At ≥ 45%: 1.37× lift. Promoted to Tier 3.
★ HardHit% confirmed as a real signal. With two days of data (n=826), HardHit% ≥ 40% holds at 1.38× lift. Drops off above 50% — same line-drive-not-carrying pattern. Graduated from Preview to Tier 3.
— Pull% held the top regression slot. Still the #1 standardized coefficient. ≥ 50% remains the sweet spot at 1.43× lift. Drops above 55%.

§ 02 / HIGHEST IMPACT

Tier 1The Big Four.

These four metrics produce the largest jumps in HR rate when used as filters. Bat Speed earned its way in this update. Note: lift magnitudes have compressed as the sample grew — these are now closer to "true" signal strength.

Pull%≥ 50% (sweet spot)

Held the #1 regression coefficient slot. Pulled balls travel toward the shorter foul-pole distance and represent squared-up barrel contact.

At Pull% ≥ 50%: 10.06% rate (1.43×) on n=636. Drops sharply above 55% (1.12×) — that's pulled-but-grounder territory.

Bat Speed ↑ PROMOTED≥ 74–75 mph

Promoted to Tier 1. After 6 days, BatSpd is now the strongest single Tier 1 lift — 13.12% at ≥ 75 mph on n=282 (vs n=192 last update). The random forest places it as the #1 most-important feature; the regression has it at #3.

At ≥ 74 mph: 11.18% (1.59×). At ≥ 75 mph: 13.12% (1.86×).

1.86×Lift at ≥75
Barrel%≥ 12–20%

Still essential as the contact-quality measure, but the lift compressed substantially as the sample grew. The elite ≥ 20% threshold dropped from 2.10× → 1.68×.

At ≥ 12%: 10.80% (1.53×). At ≥ 20%: 11.86% (1.68×). The diminishing return above 12% suggests Barrel% is best used as a baseline filter (≥10%) and stacked with other metrics.

1.68×Lift at ≥20%
Launch Angle≥ 15° (sweet spot 15–20°)

Stays Tier 1 on regression strength, even though the headline lift compressed. LA's value is in being an independent driver — it adds signal beyond what Barrel% and Pull% already capture.

At ≥ 15°: 9.35% (1.33×). Above 18° actually drops slightly (1.16×) — too steep and the ball pops up. The 15–20° band is the carry sweet spot.

§ 04 / MEDIUM IMPACT

Tier 3Corroborators.

Modest signals. Useful when stacked with Tiers 1–2 to sharpen the picture, but not strong enough to lead a screen.

HardHit% ↑ GRADUATED≥ 40%

Confirmed as a real signal with two days of data (n=826). The pattern from the preview held: peaks at ≥40% then plateaus or drops slightly above 50% — likely because high-HardHit% but low-LA hitters are crushing line drives that don't carry.

At ≥ 40%: 10.05% (1.38×). Best paired with LA ≥ 15° to ensure the hard contact is also airborne.

1.38×Lift at ≥40%
FB% (Fly Ball Rate) ↑ OUT OF TRAP≥ 45%

Reversed course this update. After 4 days it had a negative regression coefficient (a trap metric). With 6 days, the coefficient flipped positive (+0.115) and the random forest now weighs it higher than HR/FB or Barrel%.

At ≥ 45%: 9.64% (1.37×). Useful as a corroborator — still don't lead a screen with it, but it's no longer actively misleading.

1.37×Lift at ≥45%
BlastCon%≥ 15%

Plateau pattern continued. ≥ 13% to ≥ 20% all hover around 1.27–1.33× lift — pick any threshold, similar payoff. ≥ 15%: 9.39% (1.33×).

The flatness suggests BlastCon% acts more like a quality floor than a quality differentiator. Below 13% is bad; above 13% is uniform.

1.33×Lift at ≥15%
Combined Effect ↑ EMERGING≥ +5% to +10%

Now visible across 5 of 6 days (n=1,949). Some signal at the upper end of the distribution. At ≥ +5%: 8.99% (1.26×). At ≥ +10%: 9.32% (1.31×).

The HR-hitter average is meaningfully higher than non-HR (a 2pp delta), so the continuous variable carries information. The threshold sweep is just modest.

§ 07 / HONEST CAVEATS

What To Watch For.

The picture is firmer than it was at day 4, but several things deserve attention.

01 — LIFT COMPRESSION

Every single-variable lift got smaller with more data. Barrel% ≥20% went 2.10× → 1.68×. Weather ≥+10% went 2.63× → 1.83×. This is mean-reversion, not a metric problem. Expect another ~10% compression by day 10.

02 — DATA GAPS

5/17 is missing entirely. Also: 5/16 had no Weather column, 5/12-5/14 had no HardHit% or Combined Effect. Cross-day comparisons on those metrics use subsets, not the full sample.

03 — STAR TOOL DOMINANCE

The Bomb flag is doing a lot of work in the Premium Screen. If MLB's tool changes its methodology or the flag distribution shifts, the 3.50× lift could move fast. Worth verifying tool consistency periodically.
